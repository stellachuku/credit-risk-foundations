# Credit Risk Foundations: From Risk Assessment to Capital Decision

## Project Overview

This project explores how financial, business and risk information can be used to assess a company's creditworthiness and financial resilience and translate that assessment into a capital decision.

The project combines traditional credit-risk thinking with financial analysis and introductory Python.

The objective is not simply to calculate financial ratios, but to understand what those numbers mean from the perspective of a capital provider.

## Core Question

> If I were a lender or investor evaluating this business, what would I need to know before providing capital?

The framework considers questions such as:

- Is the business financially healthy?
- Does the business generate sufficient cash?
- Can it service its existing and proposed debt?
- What are the key risks?
- What could cause financial deterioration?
- How resilient is the business under stress?
- How much capital should be provided?
- At what terms?
- What conditions or monitoring should apply?

## Capital Provider Decision Framework

The assessment is structured around seven questions:

1. **Who is the business?**
   - Ownership
   - Management
   - Governance
   - Track record

2. **What is the business?**
   - Business model
   - Industry
   - Customers
   - Suppliers
   - Competitive position

3. **Does the business make money?**
   - Revenue
   - Revenue growth
   - Margins
   - Profitability

4. **Does the business generate cash?**
   - Operating cash flow
   - Working capital
   - Receivables
   - Inventory
   - Payables

5. **What could go wrong?**
   - Leverage
   - Liquidity
   - Customer concentration
   - Industry risk
   - Counterparty risk
   - Operational risk

6. **Can the business survive stress?**
   - Revenue decline
   - Margin compression
   - Customer loss
   - Higher financing costs
   - Delayed receivables

7. **What is the capital decision?**
   - Whether to provide capital
   - How much
   - At what terms
   - Conditions
   - Monitoring requirements

## Risk Areas

The project covers the relationship between:

- Credit risk
- Market risk
- Liquidity risk
- Operational risk
- Counterparty risk

A key focus is understanding how one type of risk can develop into another.

For example, a market shock can become a credit risk if it materially weakens a counterparty's ability to meet its financial obligations.

## Financial Analysis

The financial analysis will examine:

### Profitability

- Revenue growth
- Gross margin
- EBITDA margin
- Net margin

### Liquidity

- Cash position
- Current ratio
- Quick ratio
- Working capital

### Leverage

- Debt-to-equity
- Debt-to-EBITDA
- Net debt-to-EBITDA

### Debt Service

- Interest coverage
- Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR)

### Working Capital

- Receivable days
- Inventory days
- Payable days
- Cash conversion cycle

The objective is to understand not only how these measures are calculated, but what they indicate about a company's ability to absorb risk and meet its obligations.

## Python Application

Python is being developed at an introductory level and applied to financial and credit-risk analysis.

Initial applications include:

- Loading financial data
- Working with basic financial datasets
- Calculating financial ratios
- Comparing financial performance across periods
- Identifying trends in financial risk indicators

More advanced quantitative credit-risk techniques will be introduced progressively as the project develops.

## Case Study

A fictional SME will be used throughout the project to demonstrate how financial and qualitative information can be translated into a credit assessment and capital decision.

No confidential or proprietary information from employers or clients is used in this project.

## Credit Decision

The final stage of the assessment will produce a structured recommendation:

- Approve
- Approve with conditions
- Request further information
- Decline

The recommendation will be supported by financial analysis, qualitative assessment and identified risks.
