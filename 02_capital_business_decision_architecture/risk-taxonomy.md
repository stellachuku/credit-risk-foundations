# Stella Chuku Finance
# Risk Taxonomy — Version 1.0

**Parent framework:** Capital & Business Decision Architecture V1.0  
**Status:** Initial taxonomy; subject to case validation  
**Date:** August 2026

---

# 1. Purpose

The Risk Taxonomy provides a structured classification of the risks that can impair a business's:

- economic value;
- cash generation;
- financial capacity;
- resilience;
- ability to attract capital;
- transaction value.

It prevents important risks from being overlooked while avoiding duplication across the five analytical domains.

> **The five domains explain where we assess the business. The risk taxonomy classifies what can go wrong.**

---

# 2. Risk Families

The framework uses six primary risk families:

1. **Market & Commercial Risk**
2. **Financial & Funding Risk**
3. **Concentration & Dependency Risk**
4. **Operational Risk**
5. **Management & Governance Risk**
6. **Strategic & Execution Risk**

These are risk families, not scores.

---

# 3. Market & Commercial Risk

## Definition

Risks arising from changes in the market, customers, competitors, suppliers, pricing and demand/supply conditions that can impair revenue, margins or business value.

## Key Risk Types

### Demand Risk
- Declining demand
- Weak customer demand
- Demand volatility
- Cyclical demand

### Supply Risk
- Supply shortages
- Input availability
- Supplier disruption
- Input-cost volatility

### Competitive Risk
- Increasing competition
- Loss of market share
- New entrants
- Substitute products

### Pricing Risk
- Weak pricing power
- Price pressure
- Inability to pass through cost increases

### Market Position Risk
- Weak or declining market position
- Dependence on a narrow market
- Structural market decline

### Customer Economics Risk
- Customer churn
- Weak customer retention
- Deteriorating customer profitability

---

# 4. Financial & Funding Risk

## Definition

Risks arising from the business's financial structure, cash generation, liquidity and ability to meet financial obligations.

## Key Risk Types

### Leverage Risk
- Excessive debt
- High debt-to-EBITDA
- Excessive financial obligations

### Solvency Risk
- Weak capitalization
- Insufficient equity buffer
- Balance-sheet weakness

### Liquidity Risk
- Insufficient cash
- Weak liquidity buffer
- Working-capital pressure

### Debt-Service Risk
- Low debt-service coverage
- Debt service exceeding sustainable cash generation

### Refinancing Risk
- Debt maturity concentration
- Dependence on refinancing
- Limited refinancing alternatives

### Maturity / Funding Mismatch
- Short-term funding for long-term assets
- Funding duration inconsistent with asset duration

### Cash-Conversion Risk
- Weak EBITDA-to-cash conversion
- Persistent working-capital absorption
- High cash requirements for growth

### Capital Structure Risk
- Inappropriate debt/equity mix
- Excessive shareholder distributions
- Insufficient capitalization relative to business risk

---

# 5. Concentration & Dependency Risk

## Definition

Risks arising from excessive dependence on a limited number of customers, suppliers, people, funding sources, markets or other critical relationships.

## Key Risk Types

### Customer Concentration
- Large customer dependency
- Revenue concentration
- EBITDA concentration

### Supplier Concentration
- Critical supplier dependency
- Limited alternatives
- Long replacement time

### Funding Concentration
- Dependence on one lender
- Limited funding sources
- Dependence on shareholder funding

### Key-Person Risk
- Founder dependency
- Management dependency
- Critical knowledge concentrated in individuals
- Weak succession

### Market / Geographic Concentration
- Dependence on one market
- Geographic concentration
- Product concentration

### Contractual Dependency
- Dependence on a small number of critical contracts
- Weak contractual protection
- High renewal risk

## Core Test

> **Degree of dependence × substitutability × financial impact**

High dependence becomes particularly concerning when alternatives are limited and the financial consequences are material.

---

# 6. Operational Risk

## Definition

Risks arising from failures or weaknesses in the business's ability to deliver its products or services reliably and efficiently.

## Key Risk Types

### Supply-Chain Risk
- Disruption
- Capacity constraints
- Logistics dependency

### Capacity Risk
- Insufficient capacity
- Underutilisation
- Inability to scale

### Process Risk
- Weak processes
- Inefficient operations
- Lack of controls

### Systems / Technology Risk
- Critical system dependency
- Outdated systems
- System failure

### Business Continuity Risk
- Inability to operate during disruption
- Limited operational redundancy

### Cost-Structure Risk
- Excessive fixed costs
- Limited cost flexibility
- Cost base inconsistent with demand

### Operational Scalability Risk
- Growth exceeding operational capability
- Inability to scale without disproportionate cost or capital

---

# 7. Management & Governance Risk

## Definition

Risks arising from the quality, credibility, behaviour, incentives and governance of management, shareholders and the board.

## Key Risk Types

### Management Capability Risk
- Weak leadership
- Limited relevant capability
- Poor decision-making

### Forecast Credibility Risk
- Unrealistic assumptions
- Repeated forecast misses
- Persistent optimism bias

### Execution Discipline Risk
- Failure to deliver agreed actions
- Repeated strategic underperformance

### Transparency Risk
- Management deflecting difficult questions
- Incomplete disclosure
- Inconsistency between narrative and financial evidence

### Governance Risk
- Weak board oversight
- Poor controls
- Weak accountability

### Shareholder Alignment Risk
- Misaligned incentives
- Weak shareholder commitment
- Uncertain willingness or ability to provide support

### Capital Stewardship Risk
- Poor financial discipline
- Value-destructive distributions
- Weak capital allocation oversight

---

# 8. Strategic & Execution Risk

## Definition

Risks arising from the business's strategy, growth assumptions, capital allocation decisions and ability to execute its strategic plans.

## Key Risk Types

### Strategy Risk
- Weak strategic positioning
- Strategy inconsistent with market conditions
- Unclear competitive advantage

### Growth Risk
- Growth assumptions unsupported by evidence
- Growth requiring excessive capital
- Growth exceeding operational capability

### Execution Risk
- Failure to execute strategic initiatives
- Delayed implementation
- Poor track record against plans

### Capital Allocation Risk
- Investment in low-return activities
- Declining incremental ROIC
- Value-destructive acquisitions
- Excessive investment relative to returns

### Business-Model Adaptation Risk
- Inability to adapt to changing market conditions
- Slow response to disruption

### Transformation Risk
- Dependence on major restructuring
- Digital/operational transformation failure
- Execution complexity

# 9. Cross-Cutting Risk

Some risks can arise across multiple families.

The framework should therefore allow a risk to have:

- a **primary risk family**;
- one or more **secondary linkages**.

Example:

### Customer concentration

Primary:

**Concentration & Dependency Risk**

Secondary:

**Market & Commercial Risk**

### Refinancing dependence

Primary:

**Financial & Funding Risk**

Secondary:

**Concentration & Dependency Risk**

### Founder dependency

Primary:

**Concentration & Dependency Risk**

Secondary:

**Management & Governance Risk**

### Failed growth strategy

Primary:

**Strategic & Execution Risk**

Secondary:

**Market & Commercial Risk / Operational Risk**

This prevents forced classification into the wrong category.

---

# 10. Risk Interactions

Risks should not always be assessed independently.

The framework should identify when risks reinforce each other.

Examples:

### High leverage + weak cash conversion

Creates greater debt-service risk.

### Customer concentration + weak liquidity

Creates greater vulnerability to customer loss.

### Key-person dependency + weak succession

Creates greater operational and management risk.

### Aggressive growth + declining incremental ROIC

Creates greater capital-allocation risk.

### Competitive pressure + weak pricing power

Creates greater margin and cash-flow risk.

### Refinancing dependence + volatile earnings

Creates greater liquidity and funding risk.

The assessment should therefore consider:

> **What happens when multiple risks occur together?**

# 11. Risk Taxonomy vs Five Domains

The taxonomy and domains serve different purposes.

| Five Domains | Risk Taxonomy |
|---|---|
| Economic Value & Durability | What can impair economic value? |
| Dependency & Fragility | What can the business be dependent on? |
| Management, Governance & Credibility | What management/governance failures can occur? |
| Financial & Operational Resilience | What can impair the ability to absorb shocks? |
| Capital Productivity & Financial Architecture | What can cause capital or funding to become inefficient or inappropriate? |

A single risk can therefore affect multiple domains.

# 12. Risk Assessment Logic

Identifying a risk does not determine the decision.

The assessment continues:

**Risk Family**

↓

**Specific Risk**

↓

**Evidence**

↓

**Likelihood / Exposure**

↓

**Financial / Economic Impact**

↓

**Mitigability**

↓

**Residual Risk**

↓

**Materiality**

↓

**Risk Appetite**

↓

**Decision**

---

# 13. Risk Taxonomy Does Not Equal Risk Score

The taxonomy is a classification system.

It does not assign:

- risk scores;
- probabilities;
- weights;
- automatic decisions.

Those may be introduced later only if case validation demonstrates that they improve decision quality.

---

# 14. CEO Blind-Spot Application

The taxonomy should also help identify risks that management may underestimate.

Examples:

### Management sees:
"Customer concentration is manageable because the relationship is strong."

### Framework asks:
"What happens if the customer leaves, how quickly can it be replaced, and what is the financial impact?"

---

### Management sees:
"We can refinance when the debt matures."

### Framework asks:
"What evidence supports refinancing availability under downside conditions?"

---

### Management sees:
"We are growing rapidly."

### Framework asks:
"Is incremental ROIC attractive and can the business fund the growth?"

---

# 15. Core Principles

### Principle 1

> **The taxonomy classifies risks; it does not determine the decision.**

### Principle 2

> **Risks should be assessed based on their potential impact on business value, cash flow, repayment, return or transaction value.**

### Principle 3

> **Concentration and dependency are treated as a distinct risk family because they can amplify other risks.**

### Principle 4

> **Risks can have primary and secondary classifications.**

### Principle 5

> **Risk interactions matter.**

### Principle 6

> **A risk becomes decision-relevant only after assessing its materiality and residual exposure.**

### Principle 7

> **The taxonomy should evolve through real-company case validation.**
