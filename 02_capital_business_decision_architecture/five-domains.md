# Five Analytical Domains — Version 1.0

**Parent framework:** Capital & Business Decision Architecture V1.0  
**Status:** Defined; detailed assessment methodology under development  
**Date:** August 2026



# Purpose of the Five Domains 

The five domains are the core analytical dimensions through which a business is assessed.

They answer five sequential questions:

1. **Value** - Does the business create attractive and durable economic value?
2. **Dependency** - What could impair that value?
3. **Management** - Can management be trusted to protect and execute it?
4. **Resilience** - What happens when assumptions fail or conditions deteriorate?
5. **Capital** - Is capital creating sufficient value and is the business funded appropriately?

The domains are not independent scores.
They are used to develop a coherent business and capital-provider decision thesis.



# DOMAIN 1 - ECONOMIC VALUE & DURABILITY

## Core Question

> **Does the business generate attractive economic value, and are the conditions supporting that value durable?**

## What This Domain Is Designed to Discover

This domain seeks to determine whether reported profitability represents genuine economic value creation and whether the conditions producing those returns are likely to persist.

A business can have:
- high revenue growth;
- strong EBITDA margins;
- positive cash flow;

and still create inadequate economic value if it requires excessive capital or earns returns below its cost of capital.

Likewise, strong current returns may not be durable if they depend on temporary market conditions or weak competitive intensity.

## Analytical Chain

**Earnings → Cash → Capital Required → Returns → Competitive Position → Durability**



## A. Earnings Quality

Questions include:

- Does EBITDA translate into operating cash flow?
- What explains the gap between EBITDA and cash?
- Is working capital consuming cash?
- Are EBITDA adjustments recurring?
- Are reported earnings representative of normal operations?

### Key evidence

- EBITDA-to-cash-flow bridge
- Working-capital history
- EBITDA adjustments
- Revenue recognition
- Normalised earnings



## B. Capital Intensity

Questions include:

- How much capital is required to operate the business?
- How much capital is required for growth?
- Is working capital growing faster than revenue?
- What proportion of capex is maintenance versus growth?
- Does growth require disproportionate external funding?

### Key evidence

- Working capital
- Capex
- Capital employed
- Revenue growth
- Cash-flow requirements



## C. Economic Returns

Questions include:

- Does ROIC exceed cost of capital?
- Is incremental ROIC attractive?
- Is incremental ROIC increasing or declining?
- Does growth create or destroy economic value?

### Key metrics

- ROIC
- ROCE
- Incremental ROIC
- Free cash flow
- Cash conversion



## D. Durability of Returns

Questions include:

- Why are margins sustainable?
- Does the business have pricing power?
- Does it have a cost advantage?
- Are customers sticky?
- What is the market position?
- How have margins performed through market cycles?
- What happens when competitive intensity increases?
- Are current returns dependent on favourable market conditions?

### Key evidence

- Historical margins across market cycles
- Market share
- Customer retention
- Pricing history
- Competitor analysis
- Cost position



## Red Flags

- EBITDA growth without cash-flow growth
- Persistent working-capital absorption
- ROIC below cost of capital
- Declining incremental ROIC
- Capital-intensive growth
- Weak pricing power
- Declining market position
- Customer churn
- Returns dependent on temporary market conditions



## Core Interpretation

The conclusion should answer:

> **What generates the business's returns, how efficiently are those returns converted into cash, and how defensible are they?**



# DOMAIN 2 - DEPENDENCY & FRAGILITY

## Core Question

> **What does the business depend on, how vulnerable is it if those dependencies change, and what protects the business from the resulting impact?**

## What This Domain Is Designed to Discover

A business can appear financially strong while being highly dependent on a small number of customers, suppliers, people, funding sources, market conditions or contractual relationships.

The domain identifies the dependencies that could materially impair:
- revenue;
- margins;
- cash flow;
- liquidity;
- enterprise value;
- financing capacity.

## Analytical Chain

**Dependency → Degree of Dependence → Substitutability → Financial Impact → Mitigation → Residual Fragility**



## A. Customer / Demand Dependency

Assess:

- Customer concentration
- Customer retention
- Contract duration
- Renewal risk
- Customer switching behaviour
- Dependence of EBITDA or cash flow on individual customers



## B. Supplier / Supply-Chain Dependency

Assess:

- Supplier concentration
- Critical inputs
- Alternative suppliers
- Replacement time
- Contractual protection
- Operational consequences of disruption



## C. Key-Person / Management Dependency

Assess:

- Founder dependence
- Key employee dependence
- Critical knowledge concentration
- Succession planning
- Transferability of expertise



## D. Funding / Refinancing Dependency

Assess:

- Debt maturity concentration
- Refinancing requirements
- Funding-source concentration
- Dependence on shareholder support
- Availability of alternative financing



## E. Pricing / Competitive Dependency

Assess:

- Pricing power
- Exposure to competitive pricing pressure
- Ability to pass through costs
- Dependence on favourable market pricing



## Materiality Test

A dependency becomes particularly concerning when:

**High dependence + low substitutability + material financial impact**

The analysis must then assess whether mitigation reduces the residual risk.



## Red Flags

- High customer concentration
- Critical supplier without alternatives
- Founder/key-person dependence without succession
- Heavy refinancing dependence
- Uncertain shareholder support
- Weak contractual protection
- Dependence on one market condition
- Concentration hidden by headline revenue diversification



## Core Interpretation

The conclusion should answer:

> **What could break the business's economic engine, how difficult would it be to replace or recover from that dependency, and how much financial damage would result?**



# DOMAIN 3 - MANAGEMENT, GOVERNANCE & CREDIBILITY

## Core Question

> **Can management and shareholders be trusted to understand the business, execute the plan, acknowledge failure, take corrective action and protect capital?**

## What This Domain Is Designed to Discover

Management assessment is based on observable behaviour and historical evidence rather than credentials or reputation.

The central issue is **credibility as a steward of capital**.

## Analytical Chain

**Behaviour → Assumptions → Historical Execution → Governance → Response to Failure → Remediation → Credibility**



## A. Transparency

Assess:

- Does management disclose weaknesses?
- Does management answer difficult questions directly?
- Does management distinguish facts from assumptions?
- Does management acknowledge uncertainty?



## B. Forecast Credibility

Assess:

- Historical budgets versus actuals
- Previous forecasts versus outcomes
- Forecast assumptions
- Downside scenarios
- Management explanations of variances



## C. Execution

Assess:

- Strategic initiatives delivered
- Cost savings achieved
- Acquisitions delivered
- Investment plans executed
- Previous corrective actions completed



## D. Response to Failure

Assess:

- Does management acknowledge mistakes?
- Does management take corrective action?
- Does management adapt when assumptions change?
- Does management implement agreed action plans?



## E. Governance / Shareholder Alignment

Assess:

- Board challenge
- Incentive alignment
- Shareholder commitment
- Shareholder ability to provide support
- Shareholder willingness to provide support
- Governance quality



## Red Flags

- Repeated optimistic forecasts
- Management deflecting specific questions
- Unsupported assumptions
- Persistent execution failures
- Financial statements inconsistent with management narrative
- Failure to take corrective action
- Weak governance
- Uncertain shareholder support



## Core Interpretation

The conclusion should answer:

> **How much reliance should a capital provider place on management's plans, representations and ability to execute?**



# DOMAIN 4 - FINANCIAL & OPERATIONAL RESILIENCE

## Core Question

> **Can the business absorb shocks, adapt, recover and preserve future economic value without becoming excessively dependent on emergency capital?**

## What This Domain Is Designed to Discover

Resilience is broader than survival.

A business may survive a shock while becoming:
- excessively leveraged;
- liquidity-constrained;
- unable to invest;
- dependent on shareholders;
- structurally weaker.

Therefore:

> **Survival is a minimum threshold, not proof of resilience.**

## Analytical Chain

**Shock → Absorption → Response → Adaptation → Recovery → Capital Required → Future-Value Preservation**



## A. Shock Absorption

Assess:

- Liquidity
- Cash buffer
- Capitalisation
- Leverage
- Customer diversification
- Supplier diversification
- Funding diversification



## B. Management Response

Assess:

- Speed of response
- Ability to reduce costs
- Ability to adjust pricing
- Ability to change the business model
- Ability to protect liquidity
- Ability to execute contingency plans



## C. Recovery

Assess:

- Time to restore margins
- Time to restore cash flow
- Ability to recover demand
- Ability to rebuild liquidity
- Historical recovery from previous shocks



## D. Capital Requirement Under Stress

Assess:

- Additional debt required
- Additional equity required
- Shareholder support
- Refinancing requirements
- Emergency capital requirements



## E. Future-Value Preservation

Assess:

- Can the company continue necessary investment?
- Does surviving the shock impair future growth?
- Does the business emerge permanently weaker?
- Does the recovery require unrealistic assumptions?



## Key Test

A business can:

**Survive but be fragile.**

The framework must distinguish this from:

**Resilient enough to absorb and recover while preserving future value.**



## Red Flags

- Survival depends on refinancing
- Survival requires substantial new capital
- Liquidity is insufficient under moderate stress
- Fixed costs cannot be reduced
- Weak contingency planning
- Recovery depends on unrealistic market assumptions
- Stress permanently impairs the business
- Future investment must be sacrificed to survive



## Core Interpretation

The conclusion should answer:

> **If our assumptions are wrong, can the business absorb the shock, adapt, recover and continue creating value without excessive external support?**



# DOMAIN 5 - CAPITAL PRODUCTIVITY & FINANCIAL ARCHITECTURE

## Core Question

> **Is capital creating sufficient economic value, and is the business funded with the appropriate amount, type, maturity and source of capital?**

## What This Domain Is Designed to Discover

A business can have good underlying economics but still:
- allocate capital poorly;
- over-leverage;
- rely excessively on refinancing;
- mismatch debt maturity with asset duration;
- distribute capital while underfunding the business.

The domain therefore contains two related but distinct questions:

### Capital Productivity

> **Is capital creating sufficient value?**

### Financial Architecture

> **Is the business funded appropriately for its economics and cash-flow profile?**

## Analytical Chain

**Capital Required → Allocation → Incremental Return → Cash Generation → Funding Structure → Funding Risk → Forecast Achievability**



## A. Capital Productivity

Assess:

- ROIC
- Incremental ROIC
- Acquisition returns
- Capex returns
- Working-capital requirements
- Free cash flow
- Capital allocation decisions
- Shareholder distributions

### Red Flags

- Declining incremental ROIC
- Low-return investments
- Poor acquisition outcomes
- Growth consuming excessive capital
- Weak cash conversion
- Distributions despite funding requirements



## B. Financial Architecture

Assess:

- Leverage
- Debt maturity
- Debt service
- Refinancing
- Funding concentration
- Liquidity
- Equity capitalisation
- Shareholder support

### Red Flags

- Excessive leverage
- Short-term funding for long-term assets
- Excessive refinancing dependence
- Debt service inconsistent with cash generation
- Funding concentration
- Insufficient equity cushion



## C. Forecast Achievability

The proposed capital structure must be tested against the credibility of the underlying business plan.

Ask:

> **Can the business actually achieve the forecast upon which the financing or investment case depends?**

Evidence should include:

- Historical forecast accuracy
- Operational capacity
- Market assumptions
- Margin assumptions
- Working-capital assumptions
- Capex requirements
- Management execution record



## Core Interpretation

The conclusion should answer:

> **Is the business creating enough value from capital, and is its funding structure appropriate for the duration, volatility and predictability of its cash flows?**



# CROSS-DOMAIN INTERACTIONS

The five domains should not be assessed in isolation.

Important interactions include:

## Value ↔ Dependency

Strong returns may depend on a concentrated customer or favourable market condition.

## Dependency ↔ Resilience

A dependency becomes more dangerous when the business has insufficient buffers to absorb disruption.

## Management ↔ Resilience

A strong financial buffer may not compensate for management's inability to respond effectively to a shock.

## Management ↔ Capital

Poor capital allocation can destroy otherwise attractive business economics.

## Value ↔ Capital

High growth does not necessarily create value if incremental returns decline.

## Resilience ↔ Capital

A weak capital structure can turn a manageable business shock into a liquidity crisis.



# DOMAIN-LEVEL DECISION PRINCIPLE

Each domain should ultimately produce:

1. **What we found**
2. **Why it matters**
3. **How material it is**
4. **Whether it can be mitigated**
5. **What residual risk remains**
6. **What it means for lender**
7. **What it means for investor**
8. **What it means for acquirer**
9. **What should be monitored**

The five domains therefore provide the analytical foundation for the three final business-readiness decisions:

**Fundable → Investable → Acquisition-Ready**
