# 1. Purpose

The Materiality Framework determines which findings are important enough to influence the capital decision.

The objective is to prevent an assessment from becoming a long list of risks, ratios and observations without clear prioritisation.

> **Not every risk is decision-material.**

Materiality is assessed in the context of the specific decision being made.

---

# 2. Core Materiality Test

A finding becomes potentially material when it affects one or more of four dimensions:

1. **Economic consequence**
2. **Mitigability**
3. **Risk appetite / policy**
4. **Business thesis**

These four tests should be applied to every significant finding.

---

# 3. Test 1 — Economic Consequence

### Question

> **Could this finding materially affect repayment, return, valuation or transaction value?**

Consider the relevant consequence:

### Lender
- Debt-service capacity
- Probability/severity of loss
- Liquidity
- Exposure

### Investor
- Expected return
- Enterprise value
- Additional capital requirement
- Downside

### Acquirer
- Maintainable earnings
- Enterprise value
- Purchase price
- Post-acquisition capital requirement

A finding with no meaningful economic consequence should generally not be treated as material.

---

# 4. Test 2 — Mitigability

### Question

> **Can the risk realistically be reduced, protected against, structured or priced?**

Assess:

- Quality of mitigation
- Evidence that mitigation will work
- Enforceability
- Time required
- Cost of mitigation
- Management's ability to execute

The existence of a proposed mitigation is not sufficient.

> **Mitigation must reduce the residual risk in practice, not merely on paper.**

---

# 5. Test 3 — Risk Appetite / Policy

### Question

> **Does the finding place the opportunity outside the relevant capital provider's risk appetite, capacity or policy?**

Consider:

- Maximum exposure
- Leverage
- Concentration
- Sector
- Geography
- Tenor
- Liquidity
- Capital requirements
- Regulatory or mandate restrictions

A business can be fundamentally attractive and still be unsuitable for a particular capital provider.

---

# 6. Test 4 — Business Thesis

### Question

> **Does the finding undermine a fundamental assumption on which the business's value depends?**

Examples:

- Growth depends on unrealistic market assumptions.
- Margins depend on temporary market conditions.
- Customer retention assumptions are unsupported.
- Management's forecast depends on a history of missed targets.
- Growth requires more capital than the business can generate.
- Competitive advantage is weaker than management assumes.

A finding that changes the business thesis may be **Decision-Critical even when the immediate financial impact is not yet visible.**

---

# 7. Materiality Classification

After applying the four tests, classify the finding.

| Classification | Definition | Decision Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **Decision-Critical** | Could independently change the decision or fundamentally undermine the business thesis | Must be resolved, accepted explicitly, or reflected in the decision |
| **Material** | Meaningfully affects risk, return, valuation, structure or conditions | Requires mitigation, structuring, pricing or explicit decision consideration |
| **Relevant** | Important but does not currently change the decision | Monitor |
| **Informational** | Provides useful context but has no meaningful decision impact | Record if useful; no action required |

---

# 8. Decision-Critical Findings

A finding should generally be considered **Decision-Critical** when one or more of the following applies:

### A. Fundamental economic weakness

The business does not generate sufficient economic value.

### B. Fundamental repayment weakness

For a lender, credible downside scenarios indicate unacceptable debt-service or loss risk.

### C. Unacceptable residual risk

Mitigation cannot reduce the risk to an acceptable level.

### D. Hard constraint breach

The opportunity falls outside a non-negotiable policy, mandate or regulatory boundary.

### E. Business thesis failure

A fundamental assumption supporting the value proposition is not credible.

### F. Unacceptable economics

The expected return/value does not compensate for the residual risk.

---

# 9. Materiality Is Decision-Specific

The same finding can have different materiality for different capital providers.

### Example

**Customer represents 40% of EBITDA.**

| | Lender | Investor | Acquirer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary question | Can debt still be serviced if customer is lost? | Is future enterprise value sufficiently durable? | Is EBITDA genuinely maintainable? |
| Possible classification | Material | Material | Decision-Critical |
| Potential response | Lower exposure / shorter tenor | Lower valuation / higher return | Valuation adjustment / deeper diligence |

Therefore:

> **Materiality cannot be determined independently of the decision lens.**

---

# 10. Gross Risk vs Residual Risk

The framework distinguishes:

### Gross Risk

Risk before mitigation.

### Mitigation

Actions or protections intended to reduce the risk.

### Residual Risk

Risk remaining after credible mitigation.

The decision should primarily be based on:

> **Residual risk.**

However, the reliability of the mitigation must itself be assessed.

---

# 11. Materiality and Mitigation

A material finding does not automatically mean "decline."

The possible responses are:

**Accept → Mitigate → Structure → Price → Condition → Reduce Exposure → Decline**

The appropriate response depends on:

- residual risk;
- risk appetite;
- risk capacity;
- expected return/value;
- strength of mitigation.

---

# 12. Materiality and Risk Appetite

Risk appetite should be applied **after** the material risk has been identified and residual risk assessed.

The sequence is:

**Finding**

↓

**Materiality**

↓

**Mitigation**

↓

**Residual Risk**

↓

**Risk Appetite / Capacity**

↓

**Risk-Adjusted Economics**

↓

**Decision**

Risk appetite should not be used to hide or minimise a business weakness.

---

# 13. Materiality and the Business Thesis

A finding can be highly material even when its immediate financial impact is difficult to quantify.

Example:

> Management forecasts 25% growth based on entering a new market, but there is no evidence of customer demand or successful execution in that market.

The immediate financial impact may be uncertain.

However, if the growth forecast is central to:

- valuation;
- debt repayment;
- investment return;
- acquisition price;

then the finding may be **Decision-Critical** because it challenges the underlying business thesis.

---

# 14. Materiality Assessment Template

Every material finding should be documented using:

| Field | Question |
|---|---|
| **Finding** | What is happening? |
| **Evidence** | What supports the finding? |
| **Economic consequence** | How could it affect repayment, return or value? |
| **Business thesis impact** | Does it challenge a fundamental assumption? |
| **Mitigation** | What can realistically reduce the risk? |
| **Residual risk** | What remains after mitigation? |
| **Appetite / policy** | Is the residual risk acceptable? |
| **Classification** | Decision-Critical / Material / Relevant / Informational |
| **Decision implication** | What should the capital provider do? |
| **Monitoring trigger** | What future evidence would change the conclusion? |

---

# 15. Avoiding False Precision

The framework should not assign arbitrary numerical materiality scores unless case validation demonstrates that they improve decision quality.

Materiality should initially rely on:

- economic consequence;
- strategic/business-thesis impact;
- mitigability;
- risk appetite;
- professional judgment.

Quantification should be used where it improves the decision, not simply because a number is available.

# 16. Core Principles

### Principle 1
> **Not every risk is material.**

### Principle 2
> **Materiality is decision-specific.**

### Principle 3
> **Economic consequence matters.**

### Principle 4
> **Mitigability matters.**

### Principle 5
> **Risk appetite and policy matter.**

### Principle 6
> **A finding that undermines the business thesis can be Decision-Critical even before the financial impact is fully visible.**

### Principle 7
> **Residual risk matters more than gross risk.**

### Principle 8
> **A material risk does not automatically mean decline.**

### Principle 9
> **Mitigation must be credible and evidenced.**

### Principle 10
> **Do not create numerical scores unless they improve the decision.**
