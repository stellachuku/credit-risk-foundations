# Business Assessment Methodology — Version 1.0

**Parent framework:** Capital & Business Decision Architecture V1.0  
**Status:** Initial methodology; subject to case validation  
**Date:** August 2026

---

# 1. Purpose

The Business Assessment Methodology defines how the Capital & Business Decision Architecture is applied to a specific business.

It converts business information and evidence into:

- analytical findings;
- material risks and value drivers;
- residual risk;
- capital-provider implications;
- decision;
- remediation;
- monitoring requirements.

The methodology is designed to support three possible decision contexts:

- **Fundability**
- **Investability**
- **Acquisition Readiness**

However, the assessment does **not** automatically run all three lenses.

The assessment begins by establishing the specific decision context and then applies the relevant capital-provider lens.

---

# 2. Core Principle

> **The architecture defines what must be understood. The methodology defines how a specific business is assessed against it.**

The methodology therefore separates:

### Evidence

What is known about the business?

### Analysis

What does the evidence show?

### Interpretation

What does it mean economically?

### Decision

What should the relevant capital provider do?

---

# 3. Assessment Starting Point — Define the Decision Context

Every assessment begins by establishing:

## 3.1 Capital Provider

Who is making the decision?

- Lender
- Investor
- Acquirer

## 3.2 Decision

What is being considered?

Examples:

### Lender
- New financing
- Increased facility
- Refinancing
- Acquisition financing
- Working-capital facility

### Investor
- Equity investment
- Growth capital
- Recapitalisation
- Follow-on investment

### Acquirer
- Full acquisition
- Majority acquisition
- Minority acquisition
- Strategic acquisition

## 3.3 Decision Objective

What must the decision achieve?

## 3.4 Capital Amount / Transaction Size

What is being committed?

## 3.5 Time Horizon

What is the relevant investment, lending or ownership horizon?

## 3.6 Constraints

Identify applicable:

- risk appetite;
- risk capacity;
- policy;
- regulatory;
- strategic;
- financial;
- concentration;
- return requirements.

---

# 4. Select the Decision Lens

The assessment should begin with one primary lens.

## Fundability

Use the lender lens when the primary question is:

> **Would a lender provide capital, and on what terms?**

## Investability

Use the investor lens when the primary question is:

> **Would an investor provide capital at a valuation and terms that generate an adequate risk-adjusted return?**

## Acquisition Readiness

Use the acquirer lens when the primary question is:

> **Would an acquirer purchase the business at a price and structure that adequately compensates for the risks assumed?**

Additional lenses may be applied later where useful, but they should not be automatically duplicated.

## 5.5 Management / Ownership

# 6. Establish the Business Thesis

Before assessing individual risks, establish the initial economic thesis.

The assessment should be able to answer:

> **Why does this business create value?**

and:

> **What must remain true for that value to persist?**

The business thesis should identify:

- primary sources of revenue;
- sources of margin;
- sources of competitive advantage;
- capital requirements;
- cash-generation mechanism;
- growth drivers;
- key dependencies.

This becomes the hypothesis that the assessment subsequently tests.

# 9. Apply the Five Decision Domains

The evidence and analysis are then interpreted through the five domains:

## 1. Economic Value & Durability

Does the business generate attractive and durable economic value?

## 2. Dependency & Fragility

What could materially impair that value?

## 3. Management, Governance & Credibility

Can management and shareholders be trusted to execute and protect the business?

## 4. Financial & Operational Resilience

Can the business absorb, adapt to and recover from stress?

## 5. Capital Productivity & Financial Architecture

Is capital productive and is the funding structure appropriate?

The domains should not be treated as independent scores.

They are used to develop the overall business thesis and identify the dominant issues.

---

# 10. Convert Evidence into Findings

A finding must move beyond description.

For every material issue:

## Evidence

> What do we know?

## Analysis

> What does the evidence show?

## Finding

> What is actually happening?

## Interpretation

> Why does it matter?

## Domain

> Which domain does it affect?

## Decision Impact

> How could it affect the relevant capital provider?

Example:

### Evidence

EBITDA increased 20%, while operating cash flow increased only 5%.

### Finding

Growth is consuming significant working capital.

### Interpretation

Reported earnings are not converting proportionately into cash.

### Domain

Economic Value & Durability / Capital Productivity.

### Decision Impact

Potentially reduces sustainable debt capacity and increases external funding requirements.

---

# 11. Assess Materiality

Not every finding should influence the final decision.

A finding is potentially material when it can:

1. Affect repayment, investment return, valuation or transaction value;
2. Be insufficiently mitigated;
3. Breach risk appetite, policy or capacity;
4. Undermine a fundamental business thesis.

Classify findings as:

### Decision-Critical

Could independently change the decision or fundamentally undermine the business thesis.

### Material

Meaningfully affects risk, return, valuation, structure or conditions.

### Relevant

Important to monitor but does not currently change the decision.

### Informational

Useful context without meaningful decision impact.

Materiality is decision-specific.

---

# 12. Identify Dominant Issues

The assessment should identify the limited number of findings that actually drive the decision.

These may include:

- fundamental weaknesses;
- major value drivers;
- decision blockers;
- significant dependencies;
- management credibility concerns;
- resilience weaknesses;
- capital structure problems.

The assessment should not allow a large number of minor observations to obscure a small number of dominant issues.

---

# 13. Assess Mitigation

For every material risk:

> **What can realistically reduce the risk?**

Possible mitigation includes:

- structural changes;
- diversification;
- stronger capitalization;
- liquidity;
- contractual protection;
- security;
- covenants;
- refinancing;
- management changes;
- shareholder support;
- staged investment;
- transaction conditions.

The quality of mitigation must be assessed.

A proposed action is not automatically an effective mitigation.

---

# 14. Determine Residual Risk

The assessment must distinguish:

### Initial Risk

Risk before mitigation.

### Mitigation

What has been done or can realistically be done?

### Residual Risk

What remains after mitigation?

The decision should be based primarily on the residual risk.

The assessment should ask:

> **Is the residual risk acceptable for the relevant capital provider?**

---

# 15. Apply the Decision Lens

The relevant lens is then applied.

## Lender

Assess:

- repayment capacity;
- cash-flow durability;
- debt-service capacity;
- liquidity;
- leverage;
- refinancing;
- security;
- covenant protection;
- expected loss.

Output:

> **Fundability**

---

## Investor

Assess:

- value creation;
- growth quality;
- incremental ROIC;
- downside;
- management;
- capital allocation;
- valuation;
- expected return.

Output:

> **Investability**

---

## Acquirer

Assess:

- maintainable earnings;
- quality of earnings;
- commercial durability;
- operational transferability;
- management dependency;
- capital requirements;
- valuation;
- transaction protections.

Output:

> **Acquisition Readiness**

---

# 16. Apply Risk Appetite and Capacity

After residual risk has been established, apply:

### Risk Appetite

How much risk is the capital provider willing to accept?

### Risk Capacity

How much risk can the capital provider actually absorb?

### Hard Constraints

Are there policy, regulatory, mandate or exposure restrictions that cannot be exceeded?

### Risk-Adjusted Economics

Is the expected return or value adequate compensation for the residual risk?

---

# 17. Decision Synthesis

The final decision should follow:

**Business Thesis**

↓

**Dominant Value Drivers**

↓

**Dominant Threats**

↓

**Mitigation / Structure**

↓

**Residual Risk**

↓

**Risk Appetite / Capacity**

↓

**Risk-Adjusted Return / Value**

↓

**Decision**

The five domains should not be averaged.

The decision should be driven by the findings that actually matter to the relevant capital provider.

---

# 18. Decision Outputs

## Fundability

Possible conclusions:

- Fundable
- Fundable with conditions
- Fundable only at revised terms
- Not currently fundable

Output:

> **Amount → Tenor → Pricing → Structure → Security → Covenants → Conditions**

---

## Investability

Possible conclusions:

- Investable
- Investable with conditions
- Investable only at revised valuation / terms
- Not currently investable

Output:

> **Value-Creation Thesis → Expected Return → Key Risks → Downside → Valuation → Terms**

---

## Acquisition Readiness

Possible conclusions:

- Due-diligence ready
- Ready with remediation
- Material diligence gaps
- Not currently transaction-ready

Output:

> **Value Drivers → Diligence Risks → Value Leakage → Remediation → Valuation / Transaction Impact**

---

# 19. Remediation

Where the business is not currently ready, identify what must change.

Each remediation item should specify:

- Problem
- Root cause
- Required action
- Responsible party
- Evidence of completion
- Time horizon
- Impact on decision

Remediation should address the actual cause of the risk rather than merely its symptom.

---

# 20. Monitoring

Monitoring should test the assumptions behind the decision.

The structure is:

**Decision Assumption → Indicator → Threshold → Trigger → Action**

Monitoring should focus on the variables that could invalidate the original decision thesis.

Examples:

### Economic Value

- Margin deterioration
- Cash conversion
- ROIC
- Market share

### Dependency

- Customer concentration
- Contract renewal
- Supplier concentration
- Key-person changes

### Management

- Forecast variance
- Strategic execution
- Management turnover
- Corrective actions

### Resilience

- Liquidity
- Leverage
- Covenant headroom
- Payment delays

### Capital

- Incremental ROIC
- Debt maturity
- Refinancing
- Capital allocation

---

# 21. Assessment Output

A completed assessment should produce a concise decision document containing:

## Executive Decision

What should the capital provider do?

## Business Thesis

Why does the business create value?

## Five-Domain Findings

What did the assessment reveal?

## Dominant Issues

What actually drives the decision?

## Residual Risk

What remains after mitigation?

## Risk Appetite

Is the residual risk acceptable?

## Economics

Is the return/value sufficient?

## Decision Conditions

What must change or be maintained?

## Monitoring

What could cause the decision to change?

## CEO Blind Spots

What are the most important things management may not fully appreciate about the business?

# 22. Methodological Principles

The methodology follows these principles:

### Principle 1

> **Start with the decision, not the data.**

### Principle 2

> **Understand the business before interpreting the numbers.**

### Principle 3

> **Financial analysis is necessary but insufficient.**

### Principle 4

> **Evidence must lead to findings, not merely observations.**

### Principle 5

> **Materiality is decision-specific.**

### Principle 6

> **Mitigation must be credible and tested.**

### Principle 7

> **Decisions are based on residual risk, not gross risk.**

### Principle 8

> **The five domains should not be mechanically averaged.**

### Principle 9

> **The relevant capital-provider lens must be selected at the beginning of the assessment.**

### Principle 10

> **A positive decision can be conditional.**

### Principle 11

> **Monitoring should test the assumptions behind the original decision.**

### Principle 12

> **The assessment should identify what management does not see clearly about its own business.**
